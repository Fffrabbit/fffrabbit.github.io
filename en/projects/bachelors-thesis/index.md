# Utilising Text-Generating Artificial Intelligence for Learning

My bachelor's thesis is a literature review examining how text-generating AI — particularly ChatGPT — is used in learning, and what effects it has on teaching. The thesis was completed in April 2024 at the University of Jyväskylä's Faculty of Information Technology, supervised by Sanna Juutinen. The topic was very current at the time: ChatGPT had been released just over a year earlier, and there was little Finnish research yet on its use in higher education.

## Structure

The review moves through five themes:

1. **The modern breakthrough of AI** — the concept's history from the 1956 Dartmouth seminar to the Turing test, plus background on neural networks and large language models (LLMs) and how they differ
2. **How ChatGPT works and is used in teaching** — a concrete walkthrough of five use cases (explaining concepts, solving exercises, generating ideas, summarising material, language practice), illustrated with how prompt wording changes ChatGPT's response
3. **Observations from teachers and students** — benefits and drawbacks for each group separately, plus proposed solutions to the problems identified
4. **Findings** — a synthesis of what the literature showed
5. **Future outlook** — where AI's role in teaching appears to be heading

## Key findings

The review found generative AI to be a valuable tool for both students and teachers, though in different ways. Teachers benefited especially from automating administrative tasks — grading essays and writing feedback — freeing up time for teaching and engaging with students. Students, in turn, reported more individualised guidance and support for their professional development, but also raised concerns: about AI-generated errors affecting their grades, and whether too much AI support could weaken their own learning.

The review highlights a Stanford study (2023) showing that an automated feedback tool improved teachers' "uptake" practice — the ability to acknowledge and build on students' contributions — which showed up in both student performance and course satisfaction.

A recurring theme in the literature was that AI is not meant to replace the teacher but to act as a complementary tool, keeping human interaction at the core of learning. At the same time, the review identifies open questions around accountability, agency and oversight — for instance, how to ensure whose work is actually being assessed once the line between a student's own output and AI-assisted output blurs.

## Proposed solutions

The review draws together several solution directions from the literature: preserving student agency, communicating openly about when and how AI may be used, protecting personal data, and training teachers in the ethical use of AI. The thesis also compares Aalto University's and the University of Jyväskylä's AI policies, current at the time (2023), and identifies four recurring principles: students remain responsible for the content they submit, AI use as a study aid is permitted, AI does not count as a scientific source, and guidelines are supplemented at the discipline level.

*(This same question — how higher education institutions' AI policies differ from one another — later grew into its own project: [AI in Higher Education]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/projects/AI-policies-in-finnish-higher-education/).)*

## Future outlook

The review anticipates AI systems becoming more deeply integrated into learning environments through real-time feedback and assessment, but stresses that before that integration happens, it needs to be resolved how students and teachers are trained to use AI appropriately in an academic context. It recommends future research widen its literature search and bring in a national perspective, since Finnish research on the topic was still scarce at the time of writing.

## Sources and method

The review draws on international research literature (including Kasneci et al. 2023, Chen, Chen & Lin 2020, Seo et al. 2021, Baidoo-Anu & Owusu Ansah 2023) as well as UNESCO's and the Finnish National Agency for Education's AI guidance.
